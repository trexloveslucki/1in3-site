What
GBV is

Gender-based violence is any act of harm directed at a person because of their gender. It is not only physical. It includes sexual, emotional, economic, and digital abuse. It happens at home, at work, at school, on the street, and online.

In South Africa, it is overwhelmingly directed at women and girls, and the perpetrators are overwhelmingly male intimate partners. Many incidents are never reported — distrust of police, fear of retaliation, financial dependence, and shame all keep the real number out of the statistics above.

Physical violence
Hitting, choking, use of weapons, physical restraint.
Sexual violence
Rape, sexual coercion, assault, unwanted contact.
Emotional abuse
Threats, humiliation, isolation, controlling behaviour.
Economic abuse
Withholding money, sabotaging work, controlling access to resources.
Digital abuse
Stalking, non-consensual sharing of images, surveillance via phone or apps.
Femicide
The killing of a woman because of her gender. The most severe end of the same continuum.
Signs someone may be experiencing abuse
  • Unexplained injuries — bruises, cuts, or marks that don't match the story given, or that are hidden under clothing or makeup.
  • Fear or flinching around a partner, parent, or family member. Extreme caution about doing anything that might set them off.
  • Isolation — withdrawal from friends and family, restricted phone or social access, movements tracked or questioned.
  • Financial control — a partner holding all the money, limiting access to it, demanding an account of every rand spent.
  • Explaining or defending their behaviour — frequently making excuses for a partner's temper, controlling actions, or physical outbursts.
  • Sudden changes — depression, anxiety, trouble sleeping, substance use, or withdrawal from things they used to enjoy.

If any of this feels familiar — for yourself, a friend, a family member, or a colleague — the helplines on this page are a free, confidential first step. You don't have to be certain to call.

Donate pads
today

1IN3's own sanitary product drive launches with 16 Days of Activism. Until then, these established South African programmes already accept pad and cash donations. Every pack keeps a girl in school.

Dis-Chem Million Comforts
In-store · Online

The Dis-Chem Foundation runs two annual pad collection drives — March and July — at every Dis-Chem and Dis-Chem Baby City store. Participating brands match packs donated. Online donations open year-round.

dischem.co.za/million-comforts
MENstruation Foundation
Online · Cash

The largest non-profit distributor of free sanitary pads in Africa. Operates a dedicated pad factory producing the Agojie pad and installs free dispensers in girls' school bathrooms. Co-founded by Siv Ngesi.

menstruation.foundation/donate
Project Dignity — Subz Pads
Online · Cash

Funds reusable, washable Subz pad packs for girls aged ten to nineteen. One pack lasts up to five years — a single donation covers menstrual dignity through most of a girl's school career.

subzpads.co.za/donate-now

1IN3 is not formally affiliated with these organisations. They are listed here because they are established, verifiable South African programmes already doing the work. We will add our own partner-NPO channel at launch.
